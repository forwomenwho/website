Why am I avoiding a task that takes eleven minutes?

There's usually one: a form, an appointment, a reply. It would take about eleven minutes, and it has been on the list for three weeks.

There is usually one. A form that needs filling in, an appointment that needs booking, a reply that has been sitting in your inbox since the second week of September. It would take about eleven minutes, and it has been on your list for three weeks.

The task itself stays the same size. Everything around it gets bigger. Each day you skip it, it collects a little more guilt, and by the third week, opening the email feels like a confession.

What the eleven minutes are made of

Often the small thing is attached to something less pleasant. Booking the appointment means hearing the result. Replying means admitting you're late. The work takes eleven minutes, and the feeling takes up the rest of the week.

At some point you're spending more energy thinking about the email than it would take to answer it.

What seems to help is lowering the bar until it's almost embarrassing. Open it and read it, with no promise to finish. More often than you'd think, you're done before you've decided to start.

If it's been long enough to feel awkward, the other person has almost certainly thought about it far less than you have. "Sorry for the slow reply" covers a surprising amount of ground.

If there's one sitting in your inbox right now, it will still only take eleven minutes. Possibly twelve, if you reread it twice before sending.
